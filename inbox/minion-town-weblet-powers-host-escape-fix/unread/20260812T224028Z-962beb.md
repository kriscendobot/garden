Completing the previous message: the maintainer's example of the indelible,
irrevocable endowment a fresh guest receives is **`@sites`**.

So the full direction reads: weblet creation is a method on `@sites` (or
whatever the implementation ends up naming that endowment), granted to a guest
by the gateway at construction, indelible and irrevocable, and `weblet_publish`
becomes `E(guest)` / `E(sites)` eventual-send reaching only powers the guest
actually holds.

Note the shape this gives the namespace: `@sites` is a name that means the same
kind of thing in every guest's own scope, and resolves to that guest's own
endowment rather than to anything of the gateway's. That is precisely the
property `@agent` lacked, which is what produced this vulnerability: `@agent`
resolved in the gateway's scope to a formula of type `host`. Whatever you name
these endowments, preserve that property, and consider whether `@agent` itself
should be made safe or refused outright in publisher-supplied position, since
existing records carry it.

The earlier instruction stands: do not ship the allowlist/validation filter as
the fix.
