## 3. Is there a design-level flaw?

Yes. Nearly all of the churn is in one place: the **bespoke loopback HTTP control plane**. That covers port 3005, `GIT_REMOTE_CONTROL_SECRET`, per-owner HMAC credentials, facet keys, retention keys, and the `/hello` server proof. Every recurring thread (rotation, credential distribution, server authentication, caps and scans, open-by-id escalation) is a property of rebuilding exo attenuation as bearer secrets across a process boundary. None of them is a local bug. The decomplector named this in R7, R10, R12 and R14. The R14 version gave two items:
1. Replace the plane with a daemon-hosted registry shaped like `installSiteRegistry`/`site-register-caplet.ts`. The daemon owns the sidecars; `minion-git-remote` only reads them.
2. The bearer `retentionKey`, retained as `E(git).open(<id>, <key>)`, contradicts parent `designs/git-remote-capability.md` § 7: "a first-class capability object … not a bearer token". You approved that design in kriscendobot/minion.town#41.

**How it was handled, and the process gap:**
- On 2026-10-15 the arc supervisor decided to keep the HMAC layer (issuecomment-6198217929) and told future panels to treat "remove the layer" as decided. That decision came **before** R14's § 7 objection and does not answer it.
- Fix-14 escalated both R14 items to your inbox (`msg-…-fix-14-acddff7402bb`). The **proxy answered tentatively at 11:42Z on 10-16:** "rework to the daemon-registry shape, drop the bearer retentionKey; do not keep patching the HTTP control plane; re-run the panel once."
- That answer was not acted on. Fix-14 had already finished, so the reply landed with no live recipient. Fix-15 and fix-16 then kept hardening the plane, citing the 10-15 supervisor decision. Fix-16 itself says "these fixes add to that mechanism".
- The R17 pass is therefore a pass of the shape the proxy said to abandon. The decomplector does not appear in the R17 body as posted.

Whether this counts as "wrong" depends on one question only you can answer. Is a bearer secret that stays **inside** the formula and the loopback plumbing acceptable under § 7, given that the guest still holds an exo named by a pet name? If yes, the design is settled and the PR is done. If no, more fix rounds cannot fix it, because the control plane is the problem.

## 4. Recommendation

**Stop iterating. Do not grant more rounds.** The automated loop has nothing left to do: R17 passed. The decision in front of you is not "fix the nits" but **settle § 7**:

- **If the bearer key across loopback is acceptable:** record a § 7 amendment, or a one-line ruling on kriscendobot/minion.town#180 or kriscendobot/minion.town#41, saying that bearer secrets confined to process plumbing are allowed when the guest-facing authority is an exo. Then let kriscendobot/minion.town#180 ride the kriscendobot/minion.town#178 phase-ledger un-draft and give it the whole-product prod validation. Before un-drafting, **squash or regroup the 28 commits.** The integrator asked for this in R12; the history is review churn.
- **If it is not acceptable** (this is what the proxy's tentative answer and I both lean towards, given the evidence above): **stop and redesign** the control plane as a daemon-hosted registry in the `installSiteRegistry` shape. That removes the secret, rotation, facet keys, `/hello` and caps-over-HTTP, which is where 16 rounds of churn came from. Do it as a new PR, or a fresh branch built on the parts worth keeping (store, caplet exo shape, guest-control binding, tests). Retire kriscendobot/minion.town#180's control-plane commits rather than reworking them in place.
- **Do not split kriscendobot/minion.town#180 as it stands.** The churn is all inside the one mechanism, so splitting gives two PRs with the same flaw.

Also worth fixing in the garden (recommendation only; no job posted): a proxy or maintainer answer to a completed fix-N job's gating question should reach the **gauntlet**, so that a later fix round or the panel sees it. Here a "stop patching" answer was dropped and two more fix rounds deepened the mechanism it rejected.
