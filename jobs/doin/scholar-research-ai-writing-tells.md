From primary and reputable secondary sources (linguistics/stylometry writing, editor and journal
guidance, documented detector heuristics — cite them; distinguish evidence from folklore):
- Lexical tells: over-used connectives and hedges, "delve/tapestry/underscore/moreover/furthermore",
  formulaic tricolons, empty intensifiers, "it's worth noting", "in conclusion" scaffolding.
- Structural tells: relentless parallelism, uniform paragraph/sentence length, list-of-three
  everything, section-header boilerplate, "Not only… but also", restating the prompt.
- Rhetorical tells: false balance / both-sides padding, promotional adjectives, summary sentences that
  add nothing, over-signposting, hollow topic sentences.
- Punctuation/format tells and their limits (note where a "tell" is actually just good writing and
  should NOT be over-corrected — avoid cargo-culting).
