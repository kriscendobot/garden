So the question is no longer just "did a previous claimant of this job already
push something". It is closer to: before committing to an artifact, can a worker
cheaply discover that the thing it is about to build already exists, whether from
its own earlier claim, from a sibling job, from another instance, or from trunk?

Points to weigh, not a prescription:
- The three cases want different detection surfaces (job basename, the board,
  trunk), and a design that only covers case 1 leaves the expensive case 2 open.
- Cost asymmetry argues for cheap checks early. A trunk check before a build is
  seconds; discovering the collision at merge time cost six weeks on case 2.
- False positives remain the risk called out in your original body. Prefer
  surfacing "this may already exist, here is the evidence" over refusing to work.
- Case 3 suggests a pre-flight "is this already fixed upstream" check has value
  independent of any duplicate-detection machinery.
