# vat-and-compartment

Two structurally-equivalent terms across the Endo / Hardened JavaScript lineage and its 1988-2005 E-language ancestor, named at different abstraction layers. The **vat** (Concurrency Among Strangers 2005 §3) is "a heap of objects + a thread of control + a pending-delivery queue" and **"the minimum unit of persistence, migration, partial failure, resource control, and defense from denial of service."** Each object exists in exactly one vat; vats persist across the OS-process incarnation that hosts them, maintaining identity and state through a sequence of incarnations. The **compartment** (Hardened JavaScript / SES) is the JavaScript-realm enactment of the same primitive — an isolated guest-code subtree with its own intrinsics-frozen realm, providing the language-level *encapsulation* substrate that the vat's higher-level *unit-of-isolation* claim rests on. Endo's bundles + compartments together provide what the 2005 vat model articulated at the E-language level; the daemon hosts bundles, each bundle is a vat at the design level, and each bundle's compartment is the realm-level enactment.

The lineage extends backwards through Joule's *tanks* (1995) — also "the unit of separate failure, persistence, migration, and resource management" — and through the 1988 *agoric system* paper's notion of *computational firms* that Coase's transaction-cost argument predicts. Forward, the vat's role as the unit of *cryptographic identity* (Capability-Based Financial Instruments 2000 §4.2 VatID + swiss number) anchors the per-agent-keypair concept that Endo's formula-graph enforces at the persistence layer. **The cross-pillar translation matters**: design discussions that name "vat" without further qualification can equivalently say "Endo bundle" (or "compartment" for the realm-isolation aspect); the two terms denote the same primitive at different layers of the same architectural stack.

## Common confusions

- **"A vat is just a process."** No — vats include *persistence* + *cryptographic identity* + *suspension-resumption semantics* that processes do not. A vat persists across the OS-process incarnation that hosts it (Concurrency Among Strangers §3 explicitly distinguishes the vat-incarnation from the process). The closest OS-level analog is a persistently-checkpointed process (e.g., a KeyKOS domain), not a Unix process.
- **"A compartment is a sandbox."** Sandboxes are typically *all-or-nothing*: code is either inside the sandbox (subject to its rules) or outside. The compartment (SES / Hardened JavaScript) is more nuanced — it admits external code as *devices* with explicitly-named endowments and explicitly-bounded module access. The §5.3 *Paradigm Regained* 2003 *arena and terms of entry* framing is the right model.
- **"Vat and compartment are different concepts."** They are the *same* primitive named at different abstraction layers — vat at the design layer (unit of isolation + persistence + identity), compartment at the realm layer (unit of intrinsics-isolation + endowment-isolation). The library's translation block uses "vat ↔ Endo compartment + bundle" because the bundle adds the persistence + identity dimensions the compartment alone does not encode.
- **"A vat must run on its own machine."** No — a machine may host many vats, and a vat's hosting machine may change across incarnations (vat migration). The §4.1 *Capability-Based Financial Instruments* 2000 paper is explicit: vats are the *logical* unit of isolation; machine-hosting is an implementation detail.
- **"Joule tanks and E vats are different concepts."** Concurrency Among Strangers 2005 §11 (the lineage section) is explicit that *Joule tanks are the unit of separate failure, persistence, migration, and resource management* — the same role-set as E vats. E vats *further define the unit of sequentiality* (the event-loop turn boundary). The two concepts differ in detail but share the load-bearing role.
- **"Vats eliminate the need for synchronization primitives."** Within a vat, code runs serially within a turn — *no synchronization primitives are needed* for intra-vat state because there is no concurrency to coordinate. Across vats, message-passing is the *only* coordination mechanism — *no synchronization primitives apply* because vats don't share state. So the framing is correct: vats *do* eliminate the need for traditional synchronization primitives, replacing them with eventual-send + turn semantics.
