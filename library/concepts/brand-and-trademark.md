# brand-and-trademark

The family of **rights-amplification primitives** that let two references combined yield authority neither has alone: the holder of the sealer can stamp values; the holder of the unsealer can verify and recover the stamped contents; neither alone can subvert the other. The 2000 *Capability-Based Financial Instruments* §3.3 introduces the canonical form as **sealer/unsealer pairs** via `BrandMaker pair("name")` — conceptually like public/private key pairs but enforced by the *reference graph*, not by cryptography. The unguessability comes from *only connectivity begets connectivity*: the sealer/unsealer pair is reachable only by being explicitly handed out, so an attacker without a reference cannot forge envelopes. The 2003 *Paradigm Regained* §5 generalizes the pattern to **trademarks** via `interface .. guards FactoryStamp` — the holder of the stamp can mark instances; the holder of the guard can verify the trademark. The same primitive appears at multiple layers across the lineage: BrandMaker (2000), trademark/FactoryStamp (2003), Endo's @endo/marshal `makeBrand(iface)` returning a brand (modern), and @endo/exo class-shape declarations (the language-level enforcement via @endo/patterns guards).

The architectural payoff is structural: brand-and-trademark is *the* primitive that lets capability-based systems express **types-by-fiat** without cryptography. The can-and-can-opener analogy (the 2000 paper's framing) is the most-quoted: only by bringing the two together do we obtain the food in the can. Worked applications across the library: the `decr` envelope in the mint-purse-money example (Property 4 — same-currency deposit — depends entirely on the brand check); the FactoryStamp in Cassie's confinement-of-Max's-calculator example (Property: only objects carrying this trademark may pass `:Factory`); the @endo/marshal pass-style brand discipline (preserving brand identity across vat-crossing serialization). When the library names "brand" without further qualification, this concept page is the canonical anchor.

## Common confusions

- **"Brand is cryptographic."** No — Brand discipline is *language-level*, not cryptographic. The unguessability comes from *only connectivity begets connectivity* and reference-graph reachability, not from cryptographic hardness. The 2000 paper is explicit: BrandMaker pairs are "conceptually like public/private key pairs" but they are *not* keys — they are unforgeable references whose identity is established by their construction context.
- **"Brand is just a type system."** Brand discipline operates like type-checking at the surface (the `:Factory` guard rejects non-factories), but the underlying mechanism is *capability discipline*: the brand-verifier reference is held only by parties the brand-creator chose to authorize. Type systems usually require global agreement on type-identity; brand discipline requires only that the verifier and stamper share a reference. This is the "types-by-fiat" framing.
- **"The brand name string matters."** No — the optional name string (e.g. `"MarkM"` in BrandMaker pair("MarkM")) is *purely for documentation and debugging*. The brand's identity is the sealer/unsealer pair itself, not the label. Strip the name and the security properties are unchanged.
- **"BrandMaker and FactoryStamp are different concepts."** Different framings of the same primitive at different abstraction layers. BrandMaker pair is the *sealer/unsealer* primitive; FactoryStamp is the *trademark guard* primitive that does the same job through interface-declaration syntax. Both are structurally identical: a (stamper, verifier) pair where one party stamps and another verifies, with the pair's identity being unguessable to non-holders.
- **"Brand prevents forgery via global registry."** No — brand discipline is *purely local*. There is no global registry of brands; brand identity is established by reference-graph reachability. Two parties can independently create brands of the same name (or no name); their brands are *distinct* because their sealer/unsealer pairs are distinct references. The global-registry assumption is what makes ACL systems require central coordination; brand discipline does not.
- **"Endo's @endo/marshal brand is the same as @endo/exo class shape."** Related but distinct. @endo/marshal `makeBrand(iface)` creates the brand primitive (the pair); @endo/exo's interface-declaration uses brands plus @endo/patterns guards to enforce method-shape contracts. The exo-side discipline composes brand verification with input/output shape verification at the method-surface level.
