# agoric-system

A **software system using market mechanisms based on foundations providing for the encapsulation and communication of *information*, *access*, and *resources* among *objects***. The term is coined by Miller-Drexler 1988 from *agora* (Greek for meeting and market place); **the Agoric company name and the entire research lineage trace directly to this 1988 paper**. An agoric system is more than a software system that uses market mechanisms — it is one that *grounds those mechanisms* on the capability-security substrate (encapsulation of information + access) plus the resource-accounting layer (encapsulation of resources). The architectural payoff: object-orientation modularizes *competence* (what programs can do); agoric systems modularize *performance* (how efficiently they do it), with prices serving as an abstract interface for resource costs the same way message protocols serve as an abstract interface for competence effects.

The agoric vision predicts that as systems grow in scale and complexity, decentralized market-based coordination becomes increasingly advantageous over central planning — for the same Hayekian reasons it does in human society. **Agoric systems will consist of "islands of central direction in a sea of trade"**: small objects bundle into low-overhead computational "firms" (Coase's question applied to computation); cross-firm interactions are governed by market mechanisms. Production realizations of the vision exist along several axes — Agoric the company (the JavaScript-based contract-execution platform), the broader Endo / Hardened JavaScript stack (the capability-security substrate the vision requires), capability-secure languages (E, Joule, Pluribus, the Original-E lineage), and the contemporary cloud-functions ecosystem (Lambda, Cloud Run; partial enactment of charge-per-use at coarser granularity) — but the *full* agoric vision (per-object resource pricing, distributed business agents, the marketplace of mind as emergent AI) remains research territory.

## Common confusions

- **"Agoric systems = cryptocurrency."** No — Miller-Drexler 1988 predates cryptocurrencies by 21 years. *Agoric* describes any computational system using market mechanisms for resource allocation, with capability discipline as the substrate. The 1988 currency framing (§4.4) is capability-rooted, not cryptography-rooted. The 2000 paper's capability-based money is the earliest worked-out form; modern cryptocurrencies are *one* style of value-bearing instrument an agoric system might host, not the agoric primitive itself.
- **"Agoric systems = Agoric the company."** The company is *one enactment* of the broader research vision. The 1988 paper, the 2000 mint-purse-money example, the 2005 vat model, the contemporary @endo/agoric stack, and Agoric's IST stablecoin + ERTP issuer machinery are all enactments along the lineage. The vision is broader than any single artifact.
- **"Agoric systems require AI."** No — the §3.6 1988 paper argues directly that simple objects with initial market strategies (auction-based scheduling, rent-based memory allocation) suffice for the foundational dynamics. AI may *speed* the development of agoric systems (the §6.2 marketplace-of-mind argument), but isn't required for the substrate.
- **"Agoric = pricing everything."** §3.5 invokes Coase's argument that markets bundle into *firms* at small-transaction scales. Agoric systems include *both* markets *and* firms — small objects aggregate into low-overhead computational firms; cross-firm interactions are governed by markets. *Islands of central direction in a sea of trade*. Pricing is appropriate at intermediate-and-larger scales, not at every transaction.
- **"Spontaneous order is too vague to be useful."** Hayek's spontaneous-order argument is rigorous: prices summarize local knowledge into globally-actionable signals; coherent system behavior emerges from local decisions that no central planner could have specified. The 1988 paper's §3.4 application to computation is testable in principle and rests on the §3.2 *encapsulation = property rights* structural argument.
- **"Agoric is too academic / not production."** Production enactments exist: Agoric the company runs the agoric vision on JavaScript; Endo / Hardened JavaScript provides the capability-security substrate; modern cloud-functions billing partially realizes charge-per-use; secure enclaves realize the §6.1.2 opaque-box prediction. The *full* vision (per-object resource pricing, distributed business agents) remains research territory, but partial realizations are widely deployed.
