# AI writing tells and avoidance

AI-assisted prose can show a cluster of predictable lexical, structural, and rhetorical habits. A cluster is a revision cue, never proof of authorship: comparative research is model- and corpus-bounded, detector vendors acknowledge false positives, and published evaluation finds disproportionate false positives for non-native English writers. The goal is clear, situated writing, not detector evasion or deliberately degraded prose.

## Compact revision checklist

- Prefer a claim, observation, or decision over a throat-clearing opener. Cut "It is worth noting," "In conclusion," and generic topic restatements unless they add information.
- Prefer the exact relation over connective wallpaper. Keep "however," "therefore," "moreover," or "furthermore" only when it states a contrast, consequence, or addition that the adjacent sentences do not already make plain.
- Prefer concrete verbs and nouns over prestige filler. Replace "delve into," "underscores," "tapestry," vague "landscape," and empty intensifiers with the action, evidence, component, or constraint at issue.
- Prefer a complete thought over a stock tricolon. If three items are real and independently useful, keep them. Otherwise choose the number the evidence requires.
- Prefer paragraph jobs that differ. A paragraph may define a term, make a claim, give evidence, handle an objection, or state a decision. Do not give every paragraph the same topic-sentence, example, summary rhythm.
- Prefer sentence length and parallelism that fit the thought. Break a uniform cadence when the argument changes shape. Keep parallel structure when it makes alternatives or requirements easier to compare.
- Prefer a stated position over false balance. Name uncertainty, tradeoffs, and dissent when they materially constrain the conclusion. Cut ceremonial "on the one hand/on the other hand" padding that changes no decision.
- Prefer evidence and consequence over promotional assessment. Replace "robust," "seamless," "powerful," "comprehensive," and "revolutionary" with what the system does, under which condition, and what follows.
- Prefer one useful ending over a summary of the summary. End with the decision, remaining uncertainty, or next action.
- Prefer conventional punctuation and formatting that serve readers. Do not remove headings, bullets, em dashes, colons, or parentheticals merely because someone calls them a tell. Repetition and misuse are the issue, not the mark itself.

## What the evidence supports

Herbold et al.'s comparison of human and ChatGPT argumentative essays identifies rigid, repeated openings and conclusions and differences in aggregate linguistic measures. It also warns against simple quality equations: more discourse marking did not predict better logic in its corpus. GPTZero documents that its perplexity and burstiness measures describe document-level predictability and variation, not a phrase-level authorship rule. Turnitin says its own report can misidentify text and must not be the sole basis for adverse action. Liang et al. show why this restraint is necessary: commonly used detectors produced systematic false positives for non-native English writers.

## Folklore and limits

The following are not reliable tells on their own: a polished grammar, formal register, a three-item list, a heading, a transition, a long dash, a short paragraph, a cautious hedge, or any named word. "Delve," "tapestry," "underscore," "moreover," and "furthermore" are common online shibboleths, not findings established by the sources below. They belong in a revision pass only when they are vague, redundant, or out of register. A document can be excellent and still contain them.

When the question is provenance rather than style, ask for process evidence: source notes, drafts, version history, review discussion, and a candid account of assistance. Do not try to raise a detector score by adding mistakes, arbitrary variation, or ornamental punctuation.
