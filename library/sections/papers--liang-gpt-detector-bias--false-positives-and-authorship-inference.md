# False positives and authorship inference

Liang et al. evaluate common AI-text detectors on essays by native and non-native English writers. They find systematic false positives for non-native writers and connect the failure to detectors that reward or penalize predictable language. This is direct evidence against using surface smoothness, simple vocabulary, regular sentence structure, or a detector score as proof of AI authorship.

The practical implication is ethical as well as technical. Edit for accuracy, audience, and a real argument. Do not deliberately make prose worse, inject errors, or add eccentric punctuation to evade an inference system. When provenance matters, retain drafts, source notes, review history, and an account of tool use. Those are process evidence; a stylistic hunch is not.

Source: [Liang et al., "GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers"](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10364904/), *Patterns* 4(7), 100779 (2023), retrieved 2026-07-21.
