# Rigid essay scaffolding and linguistic variation

Herbold et al. compare human argumentative essays with ChatGPT-3 and ChatGPT-4 essays. Their corpus supports a useful editorial observation, not an authorship test: generated essays had conspicuously repeated conclusion openings and similar generic first sentences, and the authors characterize the realization of the expected essay form as rigid. They also report model- and corpus-specific differences in lexical diversity, modality, epistemic markers, nominalization, and discourse markers. The direction is not stable enough to turn any one feature into a rule.

For revision, treat a repeated template as a prompt to inspect purpose. Replace a generic opening with the actual claim or question. Remove a conclusion that merely reannounces the preceding text. Keep a connective only when it names a relation the reader needs. Do not delete a useful heading, topic sentence, modal, or connective simply because it appears in a detector heuristic: the paper finds that discourse-marker frequency is weakly and negatively correlated with its logic rating in this corpus.

The study is bounded to its prompts, essay corpus, and two historical models. It supports attention to aggregate patterning, not a verdict that a phrase, three-item list, paragraph length, or punctuation mark proves AI assistance.
