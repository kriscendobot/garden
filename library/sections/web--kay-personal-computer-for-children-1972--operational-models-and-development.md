## Abstract

Kay reads Piaget's genetic epistemology as a design argument for programmable media. Young learners retain practical operational models that resemble algorithms more than consistent axiom systems, and development proceeds through stages with different capacities for representation and causal reasoning. A computer can externalize those models, let a learner run and revise them, and support planning, observation, debugging, and refinement without demanding mature formal logic too early.

## Operational before predicative

The paper distinguishes operational knowledge, a strategy for reaching a goal, from predicative knowledge expressed as formal propositions. A child's practical models may be local and mutually inconsistent. That is not merely a defect. It is a developmental form of knowing that can be exercised and improved through action.

The stage account warns against pushing a later symbolic vocabulary into an earlier mode of thought. Kay uses geometry as the example: children may reason effectively about connection, enclosure, grouping, and relative motion before they grasp a global coordinate system. A learning environment should connect to those existing representations and create a path toward the next abstraction.

## Programming as an epistemic medium

An executable procedure is a visible operational model. It can be tried, observed, debugged, and refined even when it does not yet belong to a consistent theory. This makes programming a medium for practicing causal explanation and strategy. The patient and private feedback loop matters as much as formal correctness because it lets the learner preserve curiosity while testing the consequences of an idea.
