Abstract: West makes a sequence of out-of-sample forecasts and realizations an object for inference about the population predictive ability of the forecasting model. The target is a moment of a smooth function of forecasts and forecast errors, broad enough to represent accuracy, efficiency, forecast-error autocorrelation, and the average profit or utility from a decision rule. This scope is what turns forecast evaluation from a ranking of realized errors into an uncertainty-aware statistical claim.

The paper studies settings with a long run of forecast origins and realizations. Each forecast may be generated by a nonlinear or nonnested model and may use regression parameters estimated from historical data. Its examples include mean or mean-squared prediction error, correlations involving predictions and prediction errors, and a decision criterion based on realized profit or utility. The common form is a sample mean of a smooth evaluation function, evaluated at forecasts formed with estimated parameters.

This is deliberately broader than a two-model score comparison. A Diebold-Mariano loss differential is one useful evaluation moment when forecasts can be treated as given. West asks what population moment the observed forecast sequence estimates when the forecaster had to estimate coefficients before issuing each prediction. The answer is an inference framework for predictive ability, not a guarantee that any model predicts a financial return direction.
