## The setup

Word on the street is that the cost of building software is going to zero. Sounds like a good deal.

Over the last year, the author and his co-founder iteratively automated their coding loops. Each time better tools revealed a bottleneck, they addressed it. Agents would sometimes break things or propose slop, so they added more tests and guardrails. Their PRs piled up, so they automated the mechanical parts of review. When UX review became a bottleneck, they had agents attach demo videos to PRs. Nothing revolutionary, just stubborn hill-climbing on their dev loops.

By April, their agents were humming. They were automatically and safely fixing lints, nitpicks, merge conflicts, outdated dependencies, and other maintenance chores. Velocity kept increasing, and the author started filing bugs and proposing improvements they previously would not have had time for. They could respond to user requests same-day. They were working hard, but moving fast.

It was also generating a lot of Anthropic and Cursor and OpenAI overage emails.

The rationalization ran: this is the way of the future, we are a funded startup, coding costs are supposed to go to zero, and being delightfully responsive to customers is a superpower. But the overage emails kept coming, so the author finally assessed the weekly spend, and asked his co-founder how surprised she would be to learn they were spending $10,000 a week on coding. Her answer: "I have been working a lot. But that is too much."

StrongDM infamously claimed in February that you should be spending at least $1,000 per engineer per day on your coding factory. The author's team did it. The rest of the essay is about why that is too much, and how to cut the spend without losing the velocity.
