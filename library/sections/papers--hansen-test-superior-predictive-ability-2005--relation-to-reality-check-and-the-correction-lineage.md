Abstract: This section fixes SPA's place in the corpus's forecast-comparison lineage — it is the **power-improving successor to White's Reality Check** and the direct predecessor of the Model Confidence Set — and records the parameter-uncertainty boundary that limits it. The lineage runs: pairwise **Diebold-Mariano / West** (equal predictive ability, *simple* null) → **White's Reality Check** (superior predictive ability over a searched set, *composite* null handled by the least-favorable configuration) → **Hansen's SPA** (same composite null, but studentized + a sample-dependent null distribution that down-weights poor alternatives) → **Model Confidence Set** (the *set* of models indistinguishable from the best, benchmark-free). SPA was the un-ingested middle link: both its predecessor ([White 2000](papers--white-reality-check-data-snooping-2000--placement-and-the-correction-lineage.md)) and its successor ([Hansen, Lunde & Nason 2011](papers--hansen-lunde-nason-model-confidence-set-2011--relation-to-reality-check-spa-and-parameter-uncertainty.md)) were already in the corpus while the test that improves on the Reality Check was only referenced.

**What SPA keeps from the Reality Check, and what it changes.** The *framework* is identical to White (2000): `m` alternatives against a benchmark, expected-loss predictive ability, the composite null `H0: µ ≤ 0`, and a bootstrap to handle the covariance nuisance parameter. SPA changes only the *test construction*, with two modifications ([studentized statistic + sample-dependent null](papers--hansen-test-superior-predictive-ability-2005--studentized-statistic-and-sample-dependent-null.md)) that jointly make the test (a) more powerful and (b) **less manipulable** — an analyst cannot dilute a real result's significance by padding the alternative set with poor, irrelevant models, because the Reality Check's LFC-based p-value can be pushed toward 1 by exactly that padding (Corollary 1), whereas SPA's data-dependent null distribution identifies and down-weights those models. This robustness is *the* reason SPA is preferred to the Reality Check throughout the applied volatility- and return-forecasting literature.

**Relation to Romano & Wolf (2005) and the MCS.** Hansen notes that Romano & Wolf (2005) independently adopt the studentized statistic and pursue a stepwise multiple-testing procedure identifying *which* alternatives beat the benchmark; the corpus's [[data-snooping-bias]] concept flags Romano-Wolf as a candidate later node. The Model Confidence Set (2011) then supersedes the benchmark-anchored question entirely: it needs no benchmark and returns the whole set of models statistically indistinguishable from the best, doing the model *selection* that a rejected SPA test cannot (a rejection says only "some model beats the benchmark," not which). The MCS also sidesteps SPA's remaining technical drawback — the *composite* inequality null whose limit distribution depends on how many inequalities bind — by testing a sequence of *equality* hypotheses instead.

**The parameter-estimation-uncertainty boundary (the West caveat).** SPA's stationarity assumption (Assumption 1) is the sharp limit of the specific test proposed: it **does not accommodate parameters estimated recursively** (an expanding window), because that violates stationarity of the loss differentials — and for *nested* models under recursive estimation the limiting distribution is typically a functional of Brownian motions, not Gaussian (Clark & McCracken 2001; the nested case also collapses the null to the simple `µ = 0`, better handled by an EPA test like Harvey & Newbold 2000). What SPA *does* permit is **fixed-scheme** estimation (parameters estimated once, comparison interpreted as conditional on those estimates) and **rolling-window** estimation (the [Giacomini-White](papers--hansen-lunde-nason-model-confidence-set-2011--relation-to-reality-check-spa-and-parameter-uncertainty.md) route, used in the inflation application to keep the differentials stationary). This is the same lesson the corpus draws at the MCS and White nodes: **estimation-uncertainty (the [West 1996](papers--west-asymptotic-inference-predictive-ability-1996--lineage-and-forecasting-boundary.md) correction) and model-search multiplicity (the Reality Check/SPA/MCS correction) are distinct problems**; SPA addresses the second and constrains the estimation scheme so the first does not break its stationarity assumption, rather than solving it. Hansen also declines to claim optimality — unsurprising, since optimality results are rare in composite-hypothesis testing.

**The conditional-volatility versus directional-return distinction is preserved.** SPA is loss-agnostic, so a significant result speaks only to whatever loss was searched. Hansen's own examples span both sides: a variance / value-at-risk / density loss (Example 2) speaks to relative **risk-forecast** quality — this is the domain of the corpus's volatility corpus and the Hansen-Lunde GARCH horse race — while a trading-profit or point/directional loss (Examples 1 and 3) speaks to relative **mean or directional** quality. The inflation application itself is a *level*-forecast (MAE on inflation), not a volatility comparison. The benchmark-freedom and search-correction of the procedure never convert one into the other, and when the object of interest is a *latent* variance, Hansen explicitly warns (citing Hansen & Lunde 2005a) that a noisy proxy can make the empirical ranking inconsistent for the intended one — the same [Patton 2011](papers--patton-volatility-forecast-comparison-imperfect-proxies-2011--robust-loss-functions-and-qlike.md) hazard that motivates a robust loss such as QLIKE for any volatility SPA test.
