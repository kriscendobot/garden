## Abstract

The DynaBook is specified as a notebook-sized, inexpensive computer owned and carried by its user. It combines local, editable storage with optional access to networked libraries and services, treats the personal record as primary rather than reducing the device to a terminal, and supports text, programs, graphics, and audio in one medium. Portability, ownership, immediate programmability, readable display quality, and the ability to add tools are parts of one personal-computing claim.

## More than a terminal

Kay defines a personal computer as both a medium for arbitrary symbolic expression and a collection of tools for manipulating those symbols, with a way for the owner to add tools. It should improve on books in some respects without becoming markedly worse in the book's virtues. "Personal" means affordable enough to own and light enough to carry alongside other things.

The local store is not an offline cache of an institution's terminal session. It is the owner's durable notebook. A network connection can supply library material, business records, commercial media, or extra computation, but the user selects and copies material into a personally organized corpus. The paper also anticipates user-written filters over an information utility, including filters that remove advertising.

## A dynamic physical surface

The proposed machine combines a high-resolution, low-power, ambient-light-readable flat panel with a pressure-sensitive surface. The same display can present text, graphics, and a reconfigurable keyboard whose keys change with the current task or character set. These details serve the larger design: one portable surface becomes reading material, sketchbook, instrument panel, programming environment, and communication endpoint.
