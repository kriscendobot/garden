Abstract: Standard GARCH cannot represent the leverage effect because equal positive and negative shocks receive equal variance weight. The paper compares EGARCH, shifted/asymmetric GARCH, VGARCH, NGARCH, and GJR, showing that each adds a different shape constraint rather than merely another parameter.

EGARCH models log variance and gives positive and negative standardized shocks different exponential effects. GJR uses an indicator interaction so negative shocks can have a steeper quadratic slope. The comparison matters operationally: an apparent improvement may be a more plausible leverage response, an unrealistic extreme-tail response, or both. Treat the choice as a candidate-model decision, with simple GARCH as the control and a score plus diagnostic evidence as the selection record.
