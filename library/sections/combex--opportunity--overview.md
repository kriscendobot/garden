## Abstract

Combex's argument that the visible, self-advertising viruses covered by the media are the least dangerous, and that the real threat is the quietly dormant Trojan that waits for orders — making the case, via the GRC.com attack, that CapDesk and the E platform are what extreme-security organizations need. The CapDesk-era statement of the threat model the Polaris paper later carries to Windows XP.

## Content

"In the world of today, hardly a week goes by without new reminders of the vulnerability of our computing systems." The document names the Peace Vote and Nimda viruses as recent famous "successes", then makes its central point:

> The famous viruses most described in the media are, ironically, among the least dangerous of the infections: these viruses advertise their presence with their behavior, and though the infected user may find this catastrophic enough, far more dangerous is the Trojan horse that lies quietly dormant, awaiting orders from its true master on what to steal, and when.

The worked example is **The Strange Tale of the Attacks Against GRC.com**, a distributed denial-of-service attack in which, "mentioned in passing", the 13-year-old attacker "infected at least 474 desktop computers around the nation with the Sub7Server Trojan as a subtask while pursuing his main goal." The document's emphasis: "the 474 people with suborned computers were placed in far graver jeopardy" than GRC itself — the attacker "positioned himself to steal every password, capture every signature and encryption key, transfer every penny in every PayPal and E*Trade account, and essentially perform complete identity theft, from every person involved." The consequences inside an organization with extreme security requirements, "such as the CIA, are so severe that only those deep in the hearts of such organizations can begin to imagine [them]."

The pitch: "Into this environment Combex introduces the E secure distributed computing platform and the CapDesk capability secure desktop. Extreme security organizations need our solutions at the earliest possible opportunity."

This dormant-Trojan / excess-authority framing is the same one the Polaris paper opens with ("they're using the system the way it was designed to be used … The problem is the excess authority that every program gets"); CapDesk and Polaris are the two demonstrations Combex and HP Labs built against this threat model.
