The intellectual lineage of the `EQ` question — the essay the Grant Matcher Puzzle's "the implications of these different answers were *not understood* until…" link points at. Lisp introduced `EQ` (are these two values the same pointer/memory address?) for implementation reasons; giving it a meaning independent of implementation proved surprisingly elusive once dynamic object systems arrived. The story's three foundational dynamic object systems, all from ~1972, are **Smalltalk**, **Actors**, and **KeyKOS**; the latter two are foundational models of both object computation *and* capability computation. Actor semantics (Hewitt's MIT group) is a pure theory in which a reference's meaning is only the behavior it can solicit, so a reference to a *transparent forwarder* must be indistinguishable from a direct reference — this transparency is *why* Actor systems pioneered futures, lazy evaluation, and the fully-transparent Joule Channel, and *why* a pure Actor/Joule system deliberately has no `EQ`. KeyKOS, by contrast, is a security-focused operating system that *does* have `EQ` (called **DISCRIM**), the implicit or explicit basis for patterns like the Factory. Joule, designed by E-Dean Tribble, Norm Hardy, and Miller to reconcile the Actor and KeyKOS traditions, made the `EQ` question its one persistent disagreement: Norm would propose problems he thought needed `EQ`, Dean would solve them in Joule without it. The disagreement was finally crystallized when Miller, building the **Escrow Exchange Agent** for the WebMart capability-secure-commerce system (Agorics + Sun Labs), hit a problem that seemed insoluble without `EQ`; Norm boiled it down to the **Grant Matcher Puzzle**. Instances of the problem turned out to be common, repeatedly shaping the systems built at Communities.com.

Ages ago, motivated by implementation issues, Lisp introduced one of the most puzzling constructs in all of computer science, the `EQ` operation. Implementationally, it simply meant *Are these two values the same pointer (or memory address)?* Seemingly innocent at the time, when computer scientists later wished to give languages semantics — to say what the constructs meant independent of implementation — this construct proved surprisingly elusive. For Lisp, an adequate meaning was eventually pinned down, but then along came dynamic object systems. The first object system was Simula-67, but for us the story begins in 1972 with independent inventions of three dynamic object systems — **Smalltalk**, **Actors**, and **KeyKOS** — the latter two being foundational models of both object computation and capability computation.

Actor semantics, from Carl Hewitt and his group at MIT, is a foundational theory of computation (in the sense that Turing or Von Neumann Machines are). It is a pure theory of object computation and a pure theory of capability computation. In Actor semantics, a reference to an actor enables one to send messages to the actor, and the meaning of the reference is only the behavior one can cause in response to messages. If two references are behaviorally identical, they are indistinguishable. Since behavior is only solicited in response to messages, a reference to a transparent forwarder should seem identical to a direct reference. A transparent forwarder is simply an object that forwards all messages it receives to another object. As a result of this transparency, many concurrent programming abstractions were first invented in Actor languages — futures and lazy evaluation work perfectly in this context, in which the intermediate object embodying the computation could seem identical to the not-yet-computed result. Extreme in this regard is the Joule Channel: combining the power of Actors with the patterns of Concurrent Logic Languages and Concurrent Constraint Languages, Joule Channels *require* that transparent forwarders be fully transparent.

Whereas Actors came out of a symbolic-computation A.I. tradition with its emphasis on extreme flexibility, KeyKOS is an operating system (most influenced by Hydra and Algol '68) whose emphasis was extreme flexibility within the constraints of extreme security. The Joule design effort sought to reconcile these traditions and build a single system with the best of both worlds. The primary designers of Joule were E-Dean Tribble, Norm Hardy (chief architect of KeyKOS), and Miller, with significant contributions by Pavel Curtis and Chip Morningstar. Despite the difference in goals and starting points, the philosophy of computation arrived at by the two groups was amazingly similar.

The one persistent source of disagreement was the question of `EQ`. Actors didn't have `EQ`, and Joule's ambitions of flexibility demanded that Joule not have it either. KeyKOS *does* have `EQ` (called DISCRIM), and it is implicitly or explicitly the basis for several of its impressive security patterns, such as the Factory. Early on, Dean showed how the Factory could be done without `EQ`. Several years of argument ensued, where Norm would propose problems he thought required an `EQ` primitive, and Dean would then show how to solve the problem in Joule without any such primitive.

Then, while writing the **Escrow Exchange Agent** as part of the WebMart system of capability-secure distributed electronic commerce (a collaboration between Agorics and Sun Labs), Miller ran into a problem that seemed insoluble without an `EQ` primitive. Fortunately for the project, the distributed capability system they had built (originally Corbamite, later renamed Tclio) did provide distributed `EQ`. The problem became another challenge in the controversy; **Norm boiled the logic of the problem down to the Grant Matcher Puzzle.** Surprisingly, once they understood one clear example of the problem, they realized instances of it were common — deciding how to deal with it repeatedly had a big effect on the systems built at Communities.com. E ends up with two `EQ` constructs: the synchronous `==` and the asynchronous `join`. The latter sacrifices full transparency, leaving instead *cooperatively-transparent forwardability*, which preserves most of the practical benefits.
