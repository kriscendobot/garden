Abstract: In post-Treasury-Accord monthly CRSP market-index excess returns, the enlarged GARCH-M and EGARCH-M specifications yield a weak negative conditional mean-variance relation and asymmetric volatility responses: negative surprises revise future volatility upward while positive surprises revise it downward. The finding is sample- and frequency-specific evidence, not a license to transfer daily-model intuition to monthly data.

The authors fit alternative equations using monthly data from 1951 through 1989. Seasonal patterns and the nominal interest rate provide conditional-variance information alongside return innovations. They find substantially less persistence than has often been inferred from daily-return studies, and their sign-asymmetric model attributes different variance revisions to positive and negative unexpected returns.

The comparison to Nelson and to Engle and Ng matters because it demonstrates a frequency boundary. Daily evidence can find large shocks of either sign increase variance, with negative shocks increasing it more; this monthly study instead finds a downward revision after positive surprises. A risk system should therefore validate its own horizon, target, and market, rather than copy a leverage coefficient or persistence estimate from another sampling regime.
