Abstract: The simulations and macroeconomic exercise show why method-level and conditional comparisons can change the conclusion of a horse race. In simulated misspecified settings, finite rolling windows can beat expanding estimation because recent observations track the relevant local relationship. Conditional tests can detect predictable alternation in relative performance even when unconditional average loss does not separate the methods.

The application compares sequential general-to-specific selection, diffusion indexes, Bayesian shrinkage, autoregression, and random walk forecasts of industrial production, personal income, CPI, and the producer-price index at one-, six-, and twelve-month horizons. The price-index methods generally do not beat a simple AR benchmark. For real variables, Bayesian shrinkage and diffusion indexes often outperform the benchmarks, while sequential selection performs poorly, plausibly because it often selects over-parameterized models.

The empirical result is a comparison of macroeconomic point-forecast losses under the stated rolling procedure, not evidence that market return direction is predictable. Its financial use is methodological: a volatility forecaster may be compared under a variance loss, and a return forecaster under a return loss, but the target, proxy, loss, and origin-time information set remain separate parts of each claim.
