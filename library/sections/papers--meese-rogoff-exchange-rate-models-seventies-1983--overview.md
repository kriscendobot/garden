Abstract: Meese and Rogoff's foundational negative result for exchange-rate point forecasting: at one to twelve month horizons, a no-change random walk forecasts major bilateral and trade-weighted dollar rates as well as their structural, univariate, and VAR alternatives. The result is not a claim that exchange rates are exactly random walks or that markets are metaphysically perfect. It is an operational benchmark: a conditional point-forecast model needs a genuine held-out improvement over the current spot rate before it supplies a tradeable directional signal.

The candidates are three then-prominent structural asset models: flexible-price monetary (Frenkel-Bilson), sticky-price monetary (Dornbusch-Frankel), and a current-account extension (Hooper-Morton). The test also includes univariate autoregressions, a random walk with drift, an unconstrained multivariate VAR, and the forward rate. Forecasts cover dollar/mark, dollar/pound, dollar/yen, and the trade-weighted dollar. This broad benchmark matters for the finbot corpus because it separates the hard-to-forecast first moment, price direction, from the persistent conditional second moment that volatility models can use for exposure sizing.
