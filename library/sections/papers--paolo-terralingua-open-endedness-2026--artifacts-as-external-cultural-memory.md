Abstract: This derived digest, not the original paper, identifies editable text artifacts as TerraLingua's cross-generation memory: agents can create, read, move, exchange, revise, and destroy named artifacts, allowing norms and plans to persist beyond an individual agent's short context.

An artifact is a named, text-bearing object in a grid cell. It can be ephemeral or long-lived, and its content enters an occupying agent's prompt. Agents may also carry, give, modify, or destroy it. The authors characterize this as stigmergic communication: direct messages are local and transient, while an artifact is a durable, socially accessible trace that later agents can reuse or revise.

The paper distinguishes biological dynamics from cultural dynamics. Energy-bound reproduction yields variation, inheritance, and differential survival among agents. Artifact production and transformation are claimed to be intrinsic cultural evolution because artifacts can alter future norms, coordination strategies, and information affordances. The relevant claim is conditional on the simulator's design, not a proof that any persistent text store produces open-ended culture.

The reported mechanism has a practical tradeoff: longer individual context did not substitute for accessible shared artifacts in the tested conditions. The authors interpret artifacts as externalized collective memory that can reduce individual cognitive load while preserving history for reuse and recombination.
