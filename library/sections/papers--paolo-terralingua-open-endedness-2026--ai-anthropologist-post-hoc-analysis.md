Abstract: This derived digest, not the original paper, explains the AI Anthropologist as a separate, post-hoc LLM analysis pipeline: it turns agent logs, interaction graphs, and artifact histories into auditable tags, summaries, novelty judgments, and inferred cultural lineages without supplying a reward signal to the simulated agents.

The authors reject a single fixed open-endedness metric because novelty and social meaning depend on context. Their response is a mixed-methods protocol developed during exploratory runs and then fixed for systematic comparison. At the agent level, an LLM first annotates events and longer behaviors, then audits those annotations against raw logs. At the group level, an interaction graph combines co-presence, messages, parent-child links, energy transfers, theft, and artifact exchange; overlapping communities are detected before the same interpretive analysis is applied.

At the artifact level, the observer scores each new artifact relative to earlier ones and reconstructs influence links from material available to the creator, including observations, memory, and prior artifacts. Repeated samples reduce variance in novelty scoring. The resulting phylogeny is used to distinguish isolated novelty from reuse, modification, and cumulative lineages.

The non-intervention boundary is central to the paper's interpretation: agents cannot optimize the observer's criteria because the observer does not alter the environment. The authors still acknowledge that model-based annotation can misclassify events, so the method relies on explicit rubrics, raw-log references, and cross-condition comparison rather than treating its output as ground truth.

Source: [TerraLingua: Emergence and Analysis of Open-endedness in LLM Ecologies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16910), sections 2.5-2.6 and 3.2.
