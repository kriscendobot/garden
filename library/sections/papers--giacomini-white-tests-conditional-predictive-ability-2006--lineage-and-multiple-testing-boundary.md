Abstract: Giacomini-White closes the estimation-uncertainty axis of this corpus's forecast-comparison lineage: Diebold-Mariano compares two supplied forecasts, West formalizes inference for fitted population-model forecasts, and Giacomini-White evaluates finite-window forecasting methods and permits a conditional choice between two methods. It does not solve the orthogonal error of searching a large model universe and then treating the selected pair as pre-specified.

White's Reality Check and Hansen's SPA address that best-of-`N` benchmark problem. SPA improves Reality Check power by studentizing and down-weighting irrelevant benchmark-inferior alternatives, but its fixed/rolling estimation restriction avoids rather than absorbs the broader parameter-uncertainty issue. Hansen-Lunde-Nason's Model Confidence Set uses sequential multiple-model inference to return the models not distinguishable from the best, while its usual loss-differential machinery retains an estimation-scheme caveat. A Giacomini-White test can be a sound pairwise component only after the candidate set, conditioning instruments, and choice rule have been pre-specified or separately controlled for multiplicity.

Thus these tools answer different questions: conditional method choice, uncertainty from fitting, search-aware benchmark superiority, and set-valued model survival. None turns a risk-forecast win into a directional-return win. A GARCH method selected under realized-variance or QLIKE loss supports a conditional-volatility decision; it does not demonstrate mean-return alpha.
