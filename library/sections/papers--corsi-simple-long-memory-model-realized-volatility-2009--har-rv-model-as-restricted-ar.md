Abstract: The formal derivation of HAR-RV, from Corsi 2009 sections 2.1 and 2.3 -- how the three-horizon volatility cascade collapses into a single ordinary-least-squares regression, and why it is exactly a *restricted* AR(22). This is the section to read to implement the model. It defines the multi-horizon realized-volatility averages (weekly `RV(w)`, monthly `RV(m)` as simple means of daily `RV`), writes the additive cascade of daily/weekly/monthly partial volatilities, and shows that recursive substitution yields `RV(d)_{t+1d} = c + beta_d RV(d)_t + beta_w RV(w)_t + beta_m RV(m)_t + omega`. Because the monthly average spans 22 trading days, that is an AR(22) reparameterized down to three coefficients by a step-function restriction on the lag weights -- parsimony without abandoning the long lags that carry the persistence.

## Notation: realized volatility over multiple horizons

Start from the continuous-time log-price diffusion `dp(t) = mu(t)dt + sigma(t)dW(t)`. The integrated variance over day t is `IV(d)_t = integral of sigma^2 over [t-1d, t]`; Corsi reserves "integrated volatility" for its square root `sigma(d)_t`. Per the realized-volatility theory (Andersen-Bollerslev-Diebold-Labys; Barndorff-Nielsen & Shephard; see [[garch-volatility-models]]), integrated variance is approximated to arbitrary precision by the sum of intraday squared returns, so the **daily realized volatility** is

```
RV(d)_t = sqrt( sum_{j=0}^{M-1} r^2_{t - j*Delta} )
```

for M intraday returns of interval `Delta = 1d/M`. Longer-horizon realized volatilities are defined as **simple averages of the daily quantity** so that horizons are directly comparable. Weekly:

```
RV(w)_t = (1/5)( RV(d)_t + RV(d)_{t-1d} + ... + RV(d)_{t-4d} )
```

and analogously `RV(m)_t` over 22 trading days. (By Jensen's inequality the average is not exactly the realized volatility of the multi-day interval, but the difference is immaterial empirically and buys the clean AR(22) interpretation below.)

## The additive volatility cascade

Define the latent *partial* volatility `sigma-tilde(.)_t` generated by one market component. Each component's next-period partial volatility is an "almost AR(1)" in its own past *realized* volatility at that time scale, plus -- for all but the longest horizon -- the expectation of the next-period longer-horizon partial volatility (the hierarchical cascade term). With three horizons:

```
sigma-tilde(m)_{t+1m} = c_m + phi_m RV(m)_t + omega-tilde(m)_{t+1m}
sigma-tilde(w)_{t+1w} = c_w + phi_w RV(w)_t + gamma_w E_t[ sigma-tilde(m)_{t+1m} ] + omega-tilde(w)_{t+1w}
sigma-tilde(d)_{t+1d} = c_d + phi_d RV(d)_t + gamma_d E_t[ sigma-tilde(w)_{t+1w} ] + omega-tilde(d)_{t+1d}
```

The innovations are contemporaneously and serially independent zero-mean variates, with the left tail truncated to keep partial volatilities positive (an alternative is to write the model in log-RV). The monthly component has only the AR(1) structure; each shorter horizon adds the expectation of the next longer horizon -- the asymmetric propagation of [heterogeneous-market-hypothesis-and-volatility-cascade](papers--corsi-simple-long-memory-model-realized-volatility-2009--heterogeneous-market-hypothesis-and-volatility-cascade.md).

## Collapse to a three-regressor OLS model

Recursively substituting the partial volatilities (and using the identity that the ex-post daily realized volatility `RV(d)_{t+1d}` equals the latent daily volatility plus a mean-zero measurement error, which is absorbed into the disturbance) gives the **time-series representation**:

```
RV(d)_{t+1d} = c + beta_d RV(d)_t + beta_w RV(w)_t + beta_m RV(m)_t + omega_{t+1d}
```

This is the HAR(3)-RV model: a simple autoregression in realized volatility whose regressors are realized volatilities aggregated over three interval sizes. Notably, Corsi remarks the mean-zero error term on `RV(d)` makes explicit that realized volatility is **not** treated as an error-free measure of latent volatility (consistency of RV alone is not enough; unbiased finite-sample handling of microstructure effects still matters).

## Why it is a restricted AR(22)

Because `RV(w)` and `RV(m)` are themselves averages of the past 5 and 22 daily realized volatilities, the three-regressor HAR is algebraically an **AR(22)** whose 22 lag coefficients are constrained to a three-level *step function* (all daily lags share one weight, all weekly-window lags another, all monthly-window lags a third). Denoting the lowest and highest cascade frequencies l and h, the general HAR is an AR(l/h) reparameterized parsimoniously by economically meaningful restrictions. Corsi relates this to MIDAS regressions (Ghysels et al.), noting the standard MIDAS Beta-function lag polynomial cannot reproduce HAR's step-function weights. The restriction is what makes 22 lags estimable from three coefficients by ordinary least squares -- the source of HAR's signature parsimony.
