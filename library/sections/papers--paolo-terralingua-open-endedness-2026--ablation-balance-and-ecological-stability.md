Abstract: This derived digest, not the original paper, records TerraLingua's experimental claim that sustained artifact production required a balance of survival pressure, manageable cognitive load, moderate motivation, and artifact accessibility; neither abundance, maximal creativity prompts, nor long individual context alone produced the best tested cultural regime.

The experiment varies personality, temporal context, prompt motivation, artifact cost and accessibility, and food abundance. The CORE configuration uses scarce, concentrated food, short history, OCEAN+ traits, minimal motivation, and interactive artifacts. Other conditions remove personality or motivation, explicitly prompt creativity, make artifacts cost energy, make artifacts inaccessible after creation, give agents twenty-step history, or combine abundance with extended history.

The paper reports that CREATIVE produced high short-term artifact output but short-lived populations, while NO MOTIVATION ran longer but produced less per agent. LONG MEMORY and ABUNDANCE reduced both population longevity and artifacts per agent in the tested runs, which the authors attribute to cognitive load rather than simple resource scarcity. INERT ran long but had low per-agent output because agents could not reuse artifacts. CORE lies on the reported Pareto frontier by combining long episodes with steady output.

These are comparative results for the authors' finite simulation setup, not a general law. The useful design hypothesis is narrower: retain enough ecological pressure to make coordination meaningful, avoid turning novelty into the dominant explicit objective, and give agents a usable external memory rather than relying only on longer prompts.

Source: [TerraLingua: Emergence and Analysis of Open-endedness in LLM Ecologies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16910), sections 4 and 5.1.
