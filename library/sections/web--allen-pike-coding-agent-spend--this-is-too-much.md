## Abstract

The essay's central thesis about where the industry is in mid-2026. Coding agents were delivering real improvements, but $10k/week buys a pretty good human engineer, so the author cut the spend way down within a couple of days while keeping most of the velocity. The wider claim: more teams keep hitting this same transition point, and coordinating agents is less compelling once they cost more than a human doing the same work. ## The transition point every team hits

The coding agents were delivering a lot of improvements. But you can hire a pretty good engineer for $10k/week. Within a couple of days the author cut the spend way down while maintaining most of the velocity (the techniques follow in later sections).

The broader observation: every week the author hears of more teams hitting this same transition point. Coordinating agents to do something is less compelling if they are more expensive than having a human do that same work. So the field is moving from the era of "how can we use more coding agents?" to "how can we get the most out of our coding agent spend?"

This change is visible across the industry. Sam Altman has started saying that token costs are a "huge issue." Brian Armstrong is talking about how to do more agentic coding at a lower cost. Uber has thrown the model labs under the bus over agent spend. The vibe has shifted, and much of it is downstream from cloud coding.
