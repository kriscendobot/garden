Abstract: Chinta's talk motivates generative agents as an architectural response to incoherent, unbelievable long-running LLM behavior, then presents Smallville: a two-day, 25-agent sandbox in which natural-language memories, environmental affordances, and agent-to-agent conversation create the conditions for social simulation. The deck is a reading-group synthesis of Park et al.'s work, not the original research paper.

Each agent begins with a seed memory describing identity, occupation, and relationships. The environment supplies navigable places and interactable objects; agents act and converse in natural language, while users may speak through personas or directly alter object state. Over two simulated days, the population plans routines, observes changes, talks, forms relationships, and coordinates activities.

The architecture's objective is believability rather than task-optimality: memory, reflection, planning, and reaction are support systems intended to keep behavior coherent across time while still permitting local adaptation. The sandbox makes the resulting behavior visible at both an abstract sprite/emojis layer and a detailed natural-language layer.
