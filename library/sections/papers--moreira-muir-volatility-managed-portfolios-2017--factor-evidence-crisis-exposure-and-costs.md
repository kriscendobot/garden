Abstract: In the paper's historical factor sample, inverse-variance management generally improves risk-adjusted outcomes and reduces exposure during major turmoil, but the evidence is conditional on the listed factor set, monthly timing rule, and transaction-cost assumptions rather than proof of a universal trading edge.

Using monthly factor returns with daily data to calculate the prior month's realized variance, the authors report positive alphas for most managed equity and carry factors and Sharpe-ratio increases of roughly 50-100% of the original factor Sharpe ratio. They emphasize that the managed market factor's annualized alpha is about 5% in their sample, while some factors such as SMB and CMA have little managed alpha because their volatility co-moves more strongly with later returns. The managed strategy sharply lowers exposure following volatility spikes, including the Great Depression, the 1987 crash, and the Great Recession.

The paper also reports turnover and transaction-cost exercises. Lower-turnover choices, such as scaling by volatility rather than variance or trading only above long-run variance, retain positive reported alpha under its assumed costs but trade some gross performance for less activity. These are evaluation requirements for a live implementation: compare matched-risk policy returns net of costs, and do not infer that a factor-specific historical alpha transfers to a different market, execution regime, or selected strategy family.
