Abstract: The awesome-ocap PetNames wiki is a short tertiary map, not an attribution authority. It gives the familiar three-corner summary, then distinguishes keys, public nicknames, and private petnames before pointing to primary sources from Electric Communities, Mark S. Miller, Marc Stiegler, Tyler Close, and later Spritely authors. Its statement that petname systems “embody all three properties” applies to a layered system made from several name kinds. It does not refute Zooko Wilcox-O'Hearn's narrower claim about one namespace.

The page describes the desired properties as human-meaningful, secure, and decentralized. Its terminology follows Stiegler's 2005 paper closely: keys are globally unique unforgeable designators; nicknames are owner-selected public discovery hints; petnames are private bidirectional references from one holder's local name to a key.

The reference list is more valuable than the page's summary. It links a 2022 Spritely paper, Tyler Close's 2005 browser tool paper, Marc Stiegler's 2005 *Petname Systems*, Miller's *PetName Markup Language*, a Walnut capability-patterns page, a 2026 Endo demonstration, and the Endo daemon changelog. The historical sources disagree in emphasis: the early Electric Communities lineage attributes the idea to Mark Miller and others there, while Stiegler supplied the first broad external exposition and Close first applied it explicitly to phishing defense.
