Abstract: In 1980-88 TOPIX returns, negative shocks increased conditional volatility more than equal positive shocks. EGARCH and GJR cleared more of the paper's news-response diagnostics than symmetric GARCH, but the non-parametric curve showed EGARCH can produce implausibly extreme tail variance; GJR was the more stable in-sample fit.

Repeating the study before the 1987 crash weakened but did not remove the leverage evidence. This is a valuable robustness boundary, not a claim of universal forecast dominance. The empirical comparison is in sample and compares likelihood and residual diagnostics, so a modern use must additionally run rolling future-only variance forecasts, score against a suitable proxy with QLIKE, account for the full model search, and test behavior across stressed and ordinary regimes.
