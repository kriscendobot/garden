Abstract: The model universe makes the benchmark credible: theory-shaped monetary and sticky-price exchange-rate equations compete with several univariate filters and a multivariate VAR, not merely with a weak straw-man forecast. Yet theoretical restrictions, estimation sophistication, and more predictors did not translate into reliably lower held-out error.

The three structural models share a quasi-reduced form in relative money supplies, real incomes, short-term interest rates, expected long-run inflation, and cumulative trade balances. They differ in whether purchasing-power parity is imposed, price adjustment is slow, and current-account forces move the long-run real exchange rate. The paper estimates versions with OLS, serial-correlation corrections, and Fair instrumental variables to address endogenous money, rates, and inflation expectations. It also varies money aggregates and allows lagged adjustment.

The non-structural competitors include several autoregressions chosen by deterministic or information-criterion lag rules, recent-observation weighting, a frequency-domain predictor, and an absolute-deviation estimator for fat-tailed errors. A random walk with drift is explicitly tested, as is a VAR containing the exchange rate and all structural explanatory variables. The important practical lesson is that adding a flexible model family expands estimation variance and selection opportunities. A model should be compared with the simple no-change benchmark on the same forecast origins, not promoted because it accommodates more economics in sample.
