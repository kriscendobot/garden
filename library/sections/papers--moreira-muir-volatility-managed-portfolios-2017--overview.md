Abstract: This paper turns forecastable conditional volatility into a portfolio rule: reduce exposure when next-period variance is high, then assess the resulting policy against the original factor at matched unconditional risk. It is directly relevant to finbot-style risk sizing because it treats a volatility forecast as a control for exposure, not as a directional return forecast.

Moreira and Muir construct volatility-managed portfolios for the market, value, momentum, profitability, return-on-equity, investment, and currency-carry factors. Their motivating mean-variance ratio is conditional expected return divided by conditional variance. Variance is forecastable over horizons up to a year, while the expected-return response is weak relative to variance changes, so the risk-return trade-off worsens when volatility rises. The paper's central empirical claim is that managed portfolios earn positive factor-relative alpha and substantially higher Sharpe ratios while carrying less exposure in recessions and crises.

The claim is deliberately narrower than a return-alpha claim. The strategy uses observed past variance at the rebalance and stakes its value on policy performance, including costs and stability, rather than on a high-R-squared forecast of the next return. That distinction connects the paper to the corpus result that conditional volatility is more reliably forecastable than return direction.
