Abstract: Glosten, Jagannathan, and Runkle make the GJR-GARCH leverage specification an empirical route into the conditional risk-return question. Theory need not make the expected excess return rise with conditional variance: risky times can also be times when investors want to transfer income forward or can better bear risk. Thus the sign is an estimable, model-dependent claim, not a reason to assume a return forecast from a volatility forecast.

The paper asks whether the conditional expected monthly excess return on the market moves with conditional variance. Earlier evidence was mixed, and the authors argue that a standard GARCH-M recursion can be too narrow to settle the question. Their response is to enlarge the variance equation before drawing conclusions about the mean equation: seasonality, interest-rate information, and opposite effects of positive and negative return shocks all belong among the competing explanations.

That framing is a useful forecasting boundary. Conditional volatility can be a forecast target for risk sizing while conditional expected return remains uncertain. A positive theoretical risk premium does not validate a directional trade, and an estimated negative coefficient does not establish a universal law across sampling frequencies or assets.
