Abstract: This derived scholarly digest, not the original paper, frames TerraLingua as a persistent LLM-agent ecology for testing whether ecological pressure plus shared, editable artifacts can produce cumulative culture rather than isolated social simulations. It pairs the world with a non-intervening "AI Anthropologist" that interprets behavior, groups, and artifacts after the fact.

The paper defines open-endedness as sustained novelty together with retention and cumulative elaboration of prior innovations, rather than novelty judged against a fixed terminal objective. TerraLingua is intended to supply the missing substrate in many LLM society simulations: agents have finite energy and lifespans, can communicate and reproduce, and can leave text artifacts that survive their creators. The authors argue that this couples agent behavior to environmental consequences and makes artifacts an evolving medium for social memory.

The contribution is therefore two-part. The ecology is a controlled setting for studying social organization and cultural accumulation under resource constraints. The AI Anthropologist is an observational analysis pipeline designed to make the resulting volume of traces inspectable without feeding a score or policy back into the agents. The paper reports cooperative norms, division of labor, governance attempts, and branching artifact lineages across its experimental conditions; these are reported experimental observations, not guarantees of any agent deployment.
