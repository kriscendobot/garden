Abstract: Welch and Goyal's broad re-test is the central negative result for equity-premium forecasting: prominent valuation, yield, inflation, issuance, and macro predictors generally fail to beat the historical mean out of sample, especially after 1975. It does not say that all markets are perfectly efficient. It says that a signal selected from this literature needs both in-sample support and a stable, benchmark-beating rolling out-of-sample record before a trading system should believe it.

The paper standardizes a long list of published predictors in mostly linear forecasting regressions. The target is the equity premium, total stock-market return less the prevailing short-term rate. Candidate predictors include dividend-price and dividend-yield ratios, earnings-price and payout ratios, book-to-market, stock variance, equity issuance, interest rates and spreads, inflation, investment-to-capital, and consumption-wealth-income variables. Its constructive contribution is not a new alpha model but a demanding comparison contract: use information available at the forecast origin, compare the conditional model to the prevailing historical mean, and inspect when the cumulative relative error was earned rather than reporting one full-sample fit statistic.

The result qualifies the library's financial-forecasting topic. Conditional volatility can be persistent and useful for risk sizing, but a conditional expected return is a much harder object. A historical association can be genuine in one episode, yet be unusable to an investor who must choose a specification before knowing that episode.
