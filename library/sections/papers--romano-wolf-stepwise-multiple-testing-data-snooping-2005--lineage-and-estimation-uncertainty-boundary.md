Abstract: Romano-Wolf fills the discovery gap between global benchmark-superiority tests and benchmark-free survivor sets. Diebold-Mariano tests equality of loss for two supplied forecasts. West addresses the additional sampling uncertainty from estimated forecast parameters. Giacomini-White evaluates a pre-specified pair of finite-window forecasting methods, conditionally or unconditionally. Romano-Wolf instead controls the multiple-testing error created when an analyst searches a benchmarked family and wants to name every alternative that beats it. Hansen's SPA shares the benchmark-versus-many question but primarily reports a global "any superior alternative" result with a power-improving null construction. StepM goes further by reporting the significant alternatives under strong FWER control. The later Model Confidence Set abandons the privileged benchmark and reports all models statistically indistinguishable from the best.

These are complementary safeguards, not substitutes. StepM assumes a valid joint bootstrap approximation for its strategy statistics. It does not itself derive the forecast-estimation correction required when models are fitted or nested, and a Giacomini-White conditional choice between two methods does not retrospectively make a large searched family pre-specified. A credible analysis states its estimation and refitting scheme, treats any fitted-model uncertainty with the suitable forecast-comparison theory, defines the full search family and loss before selection, then uses a familywise procedure for the declarations it makes. Clark and West (2007), on nested-model predictive-accuracy testing, is the next natural source for the unresolved nested-model branch.
