# Topic: capability-theory

> Abstract: The theoretical foundations of object-capability security: the formal vocabulary that names the four capability models (ACLs-as-columns, capabilities-as-rows, capabilities-as-keys, object-capabilities) and the seven security properties (A through G in Miller-Yee-Shapiro 2003), the historical lineage of capability systems back through KeyKOS, EROS, E, and the classic papers, and the principles (POLA, no ambient authority, no designation without authority, unconfusable deputies) that operationalize the theory. Distinct from `capability-security` (which catalogs sections about capabilities-as-practiced in the Endo / Agoric stack); `capability-theory` collects sections whose subject is the *theory* — papers that argue about what capabilities *are*, what they enable, and why specific design choices flow from the choice of capability model.

This topic is the home of the library's first ingest of external (non-repo) sources: Mark S. Miller's published papers and the surrounding capability-security literature.
