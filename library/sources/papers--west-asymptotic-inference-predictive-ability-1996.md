Abstract: West supplies the second pillar of rigorous forecast comparison after Diebold and Mariano: asymptotic inference for out-of-sample predictive ability when forecasts depend on estimated regression parameters. A usual loss-differential standard error treats the forecasts as given. West instead expands a smooth forecast-evaluation moment around the population parameter and carries the estimator's uncertainty into the asymptotic covariance. The framework covers nonnested and nonlinear forecast models, accuracy, efficiency, and decision criteria such as profit or utility. It is the parameter-uncertainty foundation White (2000) invokes before applying a multiple-model data-snooping correction. It remains a comparison framework, not evidence for directional-return skill: a variance-loss application evaluates conditional-volatility and risk forecasts, while a return-loss application evaluates directional or mean forecasts.
