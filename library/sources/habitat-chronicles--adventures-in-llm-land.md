## Abstract

Chip Morningstar's 2026 personal-blog essay reflecting on a year and a half of
building software with AI coding tools, ingested **only for the portions germane to
the garden's agent-fleet-orchestration and distributed-object lineage**. The germane
core: getting value from AI agents takes *discipline*, not laziness; working with
agents is **engineering management** of a flock of brilliant-but-naive junior
developers; the central design problem is **maintaining the proper division of labor
between human and machine** — the machine makes, the human *wants* (*"the AI can make
things for you, but it can't want things for you"*); and a warning to executives that
you cannot naively swap humans for AIs in org-chart boxes, because delegation passes
down *judgment and taste* through "many layers of recursively ramified desire," not
just tasks. Morningstar frames the human/machine split as a **division of labor** —
the same term his [unum-pattern](habitat-chronicles--unum-pattern.md) essay uses for
factoring a distributed object across presences — and closes with the practitioner's
imperative: **"Learn to be a good wanter."** The skipped remainder (book cataloging,
networking debug, AI-hype commentary) is not germane and was not transcribed.
