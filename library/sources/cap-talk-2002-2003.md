Abstract: The 2002-2003 cap-talk archive is the workshop around *Capability Myths Demolished* and *Paradigm Regained*. Before publication, concrete failures and challenges force the distinctions the papers need: a Unix descriptor can still confuse a deputy when the kernel implicitly binds a numeric name; Constructor makes hidden authority and confinement operational; verification distinguishes a proven model from a bug-free implementation; normal users, not administrators, must be able to construct least-authority subjects. In 2003 the archive explicitly drafts and reviews the papers' ideas: the access-matrix thread feeds the four-model analysis; Li Gong's treatment of KeyKOS is examined as a source of the old myths; the *Paradigm Regained* review sharpens permission (direct action) versus authority (direct and indirect effects), behavioral abstraction, and overt versus covert causality. The year closes by applying that vocabulary to non-transferable capabilities: permission tokens can be made non-copyable, but authority still propagates through proxying.
