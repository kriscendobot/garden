Abstract: Romano and Wolf's StepM procedure is the corpus's direct answer to the question that a global Reality Check or SPA rejection leaves open: **which** alternatives in a searched family actually beat a common benchmark? It ranks benchmark contrasts, repeatedly bootstraps the maximum over the not-yet-rejected family, and declares every surviving positive contrast while asymptotically controlling the probability of even one false discovery. The paper makes the search correction more useful for selection without confusing it with either pairwise conditional forecast-method comparison or fitted-model estimation uncertainty. Its score remains decisive: mean-return, CAPM-alpha, VaR, and variance-loss applications make different financial claims.
