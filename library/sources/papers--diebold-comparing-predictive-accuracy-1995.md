Abstract: Diebold and Mariano propose and evaluate explicit tests of the null hypothesis of no difference in the accuracy of two competing forecasts. Unlike its predecessors, the framework admits a wide class of accuracy measures — the loss function need not be quadratic and need not even be symmetric — and the forecast errors may be non-Gaussian, nonzero-mean, serially correlated, and contemporaneously correlated. The central object is the loss differential `d_t = g(e_1t) − g(e_2t)`, and equal accuracy reduces to `E[d_t] = 0`. The paper gives a large-sample statistic (`S1`, the mean loss differential over its long-run standard error), exact finite-sample sign and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, a Monte Carlo size comparison against the classical quadratic-loss tests (`F`, MGN, MR), and an exchange-rate application. It is the foundational, loss-agnostic forecast-comparison test and the pairwise ancestor of White's Reality Check, Hansen's SPA, and the Model Confidence Set.
