Abstract: The canonical broad audit of **equity-premium return prediction**. Welch and Goyal re-evaluate the prominent published valuation, issuance, yield, interest-rate, inflation, and macro predictors under common samples and rolling out-of-sample procedures. Their central result is negative but operationally valuable: most models are unstable, often derive apparent success from the 1973-1975 Oil Shock, and fail to beat a prevailing historical-mean forecast after 1975. The paper fixes a standard for any claimed return signal: name the unconditional benchmark, make each forecast using only information then available, report the full rolling out-of-sample record and its subperiods, and account for the many specifications searched before selecting a model.
