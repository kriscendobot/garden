Abstract: The 2004-2008 cap-talk era moves from capability-model definition into deployable object-capability patterns. CapDesk and Polaris test dynamic least authority on ordinary desktop applications; Firefox IDN spoofing drives the petname UI; password capabilities become web-keys; and Waterken's `ref_send` brings eventual references to Java. Later threads sharpen resource accounting, facets, deep attenuation, accountability, formal pattern analysis, persistent ocap languages, browser sandboxing, and the boundary between dependency injection and enforced capability discipline. The list also reopens hard boundaries: whether wrappers can mediate an arbitrary interface, whether authority is monotonic, how ACLs may coexist with ocaps, and whether non-delegation is useful. This source index cross-links those workshop discussions to the Miller, Close, and Polaris papers that later give them a stable published shape.
