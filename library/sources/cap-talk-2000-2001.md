Abstract: The 2000-2001 cap-talk archive is quieter than 1998-1999 but sharpens the representation-and-protocol questions the founding years opened. The 2000 threads settle the vocabulary of capability *representation* (partitioned, tagged, and password/sparse capabilities) and tie it to confinement (Shapiro's SW model versus E's observable immutability), restate the practical KeyKOS/EROS model for newcomers (persistence, no root, creator-holds-the-first-key, copied-not-delegated, revocation by interposed nullifiable indirection), and refine the trusted-computing-base into a per-application rather than a singular notion. The 2001 threads turn outward to the web: a standard off-line capability *representation* is distinguished from an on-line capability *protocol* (SPKI, E's cap:// URI and Pluribus, Waterken's https encoding), a home-rolled design (Hanson's Goo) is reviewed against the "why not SPKI" bar, Miller sets down the two-threads-of-capability-thinking history (operating systems versus the lambda calculus), and David Nicol catalogues the bearer certificates already pervasive in everyday life.
