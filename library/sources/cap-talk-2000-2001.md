Abstract: The 2000-2001 cap-talk archive is quieter than 1998-1999 but sharpens the representation-and-protocol questions the founding years opened. The 2000 threads settle the vocabulary of capability *representation* (partitioned, tagged, and password/sparse capabilities) and tie it to confinement (Shapiro's SW model versus E's observable immutability), restate the practical KeyKOS/EROS model for newcomers, and refine the trusted-computing-base into a per-application rather than a singular notion. A recovered March thread works through web capability leakage and session-scoped revocation; a recovered October thread demonstrates that use-once tokens cannot constrain delegation because a holder can wrap refreshed tokens into a reusable proxy. The 2001 threads turn outward to the web: a standard off-line capability *representation* is distinguished from an on-line capability *protocol*, Miller sets down the two-threads-of-capability-thinking history, and David Nicol catalogues the bearer certificates already pervasive in everyday life.
