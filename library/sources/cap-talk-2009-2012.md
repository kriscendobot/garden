Abstract: The 2009-2012 cap-talk archive is the object-capability community's move onto the mainstream web platform. The load-bearing questions of the era are web-shaped: whether ACLs can secure a multi-user Web at all (Tyler Close's "ACLs don't", refuting the access-matrix equivalence a decade after Shapiro first argued it on this same list), how a browser can hold authority when a capability *is* a URL (the web-key / powerbox problem), and why CSRF, clickjacking, and click-fraud are all the confused deputy wearing web clothes. In parallel the JavaScript-subset systems that become SES and Endo — Cajita and the Caja lineage — take their place in a taxonomy of live ocap systems next to E, Joe-E, and the seL4/CapROS microkernels. This first slice covers 2009 Q1; later 2009 and 2010-2012 are deferred to a remainder cycle.
