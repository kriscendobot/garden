Abstract: The 2009-2012 cap-talk archive is the object-capability community's move onto the mainstream web platform. The load-bearing questions of the era are web-shaped: whether ACLs can secure a multi-user Web at all (Tyler Close's "ACLs don't", refuting the access-matrix equivalence a decade after Shapiro first argued it on this same list), how a browser can hold authority when a capability *is* a URL (the web-key / powerbox problem), and why CSRF, clickjacking, and click-fraud are all the confused deputy wearing web clothes. In parallel the JavaScript-subset systems that become SES and Endo (Cajita and the Caja lineage) take their place in a taxonomy of live ocap systems next to E, Joe-E, and the seL4/CapROS microkernels. All of 2009 and 2010 are anchored and sectioned. January-March 2011 add service-chain adoption, precise petname and Zooko-triangle mappings, the copy-data versus authority boundary, covert-channel limits, Waterken's transactional persistence failure model, and the seL4 verification boundary. April-July 2011 are now sectioned too: the long immutable-data / sealed-value debate, type-passing and rights amplification, self-authenticating YURL hash length, GC versus RAII, the provability of defensive correctness, ZBAC versus capabilities, chained-access delegation, re-authentication as time-limited capabilities, and Sitelier's user-owned web OS. August-December 2011 and January-March 2012 are anchored and surveyed but not yet sectioned; the rest of 2012 (and October, unavailable via the Internet Archive after seven attempts) remain for further cycles.
